Skill points explained

Skill points decide which items you can wear and how hard your elements hit. Most "this build doesn't work" problems are skill point problems, so it is worth knowing exactly how they add up.

The five skills

Each skill belongs to one element. In the damage model a skill raises its element's damage; three of them also do something more:

SkillElementAlso
StrengthEarthmultiplies all of your damage, not only earth
DexterityThundergives the chance of a critical hit
IntelligenceWater-
DefenceFirereduces the damage you take
AgilityAirlets part of each hit mostly miss you (counted as an average)

That is why so many builds put points into Strength and Dexterity even when their main element is something else: both of them improve every hit.

How many points you have

You get 2 skill points per level from level 2 onwards, which caps at 200 points at level 101. After that, levels no longer add skill points.

Level10305080100101+
Skill points185898158198200

You can put at most 100 of your own points into one skill. Bonuses from items (for example "+7 Strength") come on top of that and can take a skill above 100.

What a point is worth: the curve

Skill points have diminishing returns. The bonus from n points is

bonus(n) = r / (1 - r) × (1 - r^n) %, r = 0.9908, n counted up to 150

The first point is worth almost 1%, the hundredth only about 0.4%, and past 150 points nothing more is added:

Points10205080100120150
Bonus9.5%18.2%39.9%56.3%65.0%72.2%80.8%

Strength, Dexterity and Intelligence use this bonus as it is. Defence uses 86.7% of it and Agility 95.1% of it, so 100 Defence reduces damage by about 56% and 100 Agility covers about 62% of a hit. The practical lesson: going from 0 to 50 points in a skill buys far more than going from 100 to 150, so spreading points over two or three skills is often stronger than maxing one.

Requirements and item bonuses

Most items above the early levels have skill requirements, for example "Strength Min: 45". Your assigned points plus the bonuses of the other items you wear have to meet it. Bonuses therefore do two jobs: they add damage (a +10 Dexterity ring is +10 Dexterity for the curve above) and they pay part of the requirements of other items, which frees your own points for something else.

Negative bonuses work the other way: an item with "-15 Agility" raises what you have to assign to keep your Agility items working. A build can look fine on paper and still be impossible because of two or three negative bonuses stacked together.

Equip order

In the game you put items on one at a time, and the requirement of each item is checked with what you have at that moment. An item that gives the Strength another item needs has to go on first. Wynnbuilder - and this site - search for an order in which all nine items can be equipped and none of them stops meeting its requirement once the rest are on. If no such order exists, the build doesn't fit, however many free points it seems to leave.

How the site handles them

  • Every generated build is validated in the game's equip order. A build that doesn't fit your level's points is never presented as valid.
  • Points the items don't need are spent where they raise your goal the most, and shown as +X next to the skill. You can turn that off if you prefer to spend them yourself.
  • In the Build Creator you assign points by hand; going over your budget, or over 100 in one skill, shows a warning instead of blocking you.
  • Guild tomes that give skill points are counted as bonuses, like item bonuses.
